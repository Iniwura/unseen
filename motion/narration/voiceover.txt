Most release failures don't start with broken code. They start with information nobody connected.

A build can pass every routine test while a critical rule sits in another team's conversation.

UNSEEN closes that gap.

For this checkout release, engineering submitted an exact Git commit to Switch. The baseline passed.

But support had an overlooked rule: total refunds must never exceed the payment captured.

UNSEEN retrieved that evidence, ran an independent regression against the submitted commit, and exposed the failure: a second refund could push the total past the limit.

The gate closed. No approval by assumption.

Engineering fixed the bug at a new commit. UNSEEN verified that exact revision, reran the tests, and required fresh evidence from both teams.

Only after those checks did a human authorize the verified release preview.

Every decision is traceable. Nothing quietly bypasses the gate.

UNSEEN. The release gate that asks for proof, before permission.
